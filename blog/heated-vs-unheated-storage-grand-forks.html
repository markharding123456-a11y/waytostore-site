Heated or unheated storage: what actually needs the heat.

Heated storage costs more than a cold unit or a spot in the yard. For some things it is money well spent. For others it is wasted. Here is how to tell which is which.

What cold does

Two things happen to belongings in an unheated space over a Boundary winter. Anything containing water freezes and expands. And every time the temperature swings, moisture in the air condenses on cold surfaces, then dries, then condenses again. Freezing cracks things. The wet-dry cycle warps wood, spots fabric, corrodes metal and gets inside electronics. A heated building holds the temperature above freezing and takes most of the swing out of the cycle. It is not a humidity-controlled vault, so you still pack sensibly, but it removes the two forces that do the damage.

Belongs in a heated unit

  • Anything with a screen or a circuit board. Televisions, computers, printers, stereo equipment, power tools with batteries. Condensation is what kills them.
  • Wood furniture and instruments. Solid wood, veneer, pianos and guitars move with temperature and humidity. Joints loosen, veneer lifts, finishes crack.
  • Upholstery, mattresses, leather and clothing. Damp plus cold plus time equals mildew and a smell that does not leave.
  • Paper and photographs. Documents, books, photo albums, artwork. They absorb moisture, stick together and foxing sets in.
  • Liquids you are allowed to store. Cosmetics, cleaning products, water-based paint and anything in a bottle will freeze and split. Note that fuel, propane, solvents and other hazardous materials are not allowed in any unit, heated or not.
  • Business records and inventory. If you would be upset to open the box and find it damp, it goes inside.

Fine in the compound or a cold unit

  • Vehicles, RVs, boats and trailers that have been winterized. See our winter checklist.
  • Metal tools without batteries, lightly oiled.
  • Plastic bins of hardware, camping gear, sports equipment, garden tools.
  • Patio furniture, ladders, lumber, building materials, tires.
  • A shipping container you own, and whatever you have packed inside it to your own standard.

Packing for a heated unit, so it stays that way

Heat keeps the building above freezing. Your packing does the rest.

  1. Boxes off the concrete. Pallets, a plastic sheet, or a layer of two-by-fours.
  2. Wrap upholstered furniture and mattresses in breathable covers, not plastic. Plastic traps the moisture already in the fabric.
  3. Leave an inch between stacked items and the wall so air moves.
  4. Drain and dry anything that has held water before it goes in: washers, coolers, garden hoses.
  5. A couple of moisture absorbers in a sealed unit go a long way for the cost.

The honest answer

If everything you are storing is on the second list, save your money and take a cold space. If anything is on the first list, it is worth the difference.
